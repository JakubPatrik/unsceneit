## The angle

Music videos are where the subtler revealing and sexualized content lives (STORY.md). The extension blurs that content, and with "Mute sound while hidden" switched off, **the song keeps playing**. That's something a skip button or closing the tab can't do, and it shows that the filters are yours to set.

## Hooks to test

The first draft's hook ("Everyone knows this song. Nobody plays the video with their parents in the room.") was too long and too flat. Stronger ones, best first:

1. **"POV: you put on Gata Only and your partner walks in."** Open on your face, glance off camera, then whip to the laptop as the blur hits. It's a familiar format on Shorts, it names the song people search for, and the blur is the punchline. Follow it with "Relax. I built a Chrome extension for YouTube that blurs that part." That replaces the 2–8s lines.
2. **"Keep the song. Lose the video."** Four words. Open on the blurred video with the music playing, then cut to you.
3. **"Play this music video at a family dinner. I dare you."** A dare holds viewers, and the payoff is you doing it with the blur on.

## Keep it accurate

- **Describe what gets hidden, never moralize.** Say "revealing" and "sexualized", never "dirty" or "inappropriate". Don't single out dancing (the founder's call, and the lint warns about it).
- **Say "blurs", never "skips".**
- **"The song never stops"** is only true with "Mute sound while hidden" off. It's on by default, so show the switch.
- **Not "live".** The demo plays from the library.
- **"YouTube in Chrome"** goes on screen at the end.
- **The outro says "free".** That's true during the alpha. If pricing changes before this posts, say "free to try".
- **Say "Chrome Web Store", not "link in bio",** unless the link is actually in the channel's bio.

## Shooting

- **Copyright is the big risk here.** The song is a major-label track, and YouTube's Content ID matches audio. A reel with the song audible will almost certainly be claimed, which can mean it's blocked in some countries or demonetized. Keep the song low under your voice, and keep the demo short.
- Keep talking over the demo, with no silent stretches.
